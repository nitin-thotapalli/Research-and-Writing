## Executive Summary & Preliminary Business Assessment

**Working assessment:** Suzlon is an established, vertically integrated Indian wind turbine manufacturer expanding into a broader life-cycle renewable-energy model: technology, project development support, EPC/project execution and asset management. It has a substantial manufacturing and installed-service footprint, improving delivery volumes and significant contracted order visibility. However, a large book of orders does not by itself establish completed projects, future cash generation or durable pricing power. [Suzlon Q2 FY27 / C01](https://www.suzlon.com/media/press/press-releases/suzlon-q2-revenue-grows-26-yo-y-on-record-deliveries/) · [Suzlon businesses / C02](https://www.suzlon.com/our-businesses/)

- **Core product scale:** Suzlon reports 4,500 MW annual domestic wind-turbine manufacturing capacity across 15 specialised hubs as of its October 2026 update. This is nominal annual capacity, not proof of that year's output. [C01](https://www.suzlon.com/media/press/press-releases/suzlon-q2-revenue-grows-26-yo-y-on-record-deliveries/)
- **Order visibility:** Management described a 6+ GW order book in October 2026 and over 1.5 GW new orders received in H1 FY27; 38% of the order book was EPC-scoped. These are company-defined order quantities, not revenue or installed electricity capacity. [C01](https://www.suzlon.com/media/press/press-releases/suzlon-q2-revenue-grows-26-yo-y-on-record-deliveries/)
- **Delivery versus commissioning:** Q2 FY27 India turbine deliveries were 710 MW, while project commissioning was 454 MW. H1 commissioning was 723 MW. Distinguishing physical shipment/delivery from grid-ready commissioning is fundamental. [C01](https://www.suzlon.com/media/press/press-releases/suzlon-q2-revenue-grows-26-yo-y-on-record-deliveries/)
- **Service franchise:** Suzlon reports 16,000+ MW in its India asset management/service footprint, 9,600+ turbines at 110+ sites and 1,900+ customers; these are operational footprint metrics, not disclosed recurring revenue. [C01](https://www.suzlon.com/media/press/press-releases/suzlon-q2-revenue-grows-26-yo-y-on-record-deliveries/)
- **Competitive reality:** JMK's FY2026 ranking by *commissioned wind capacity that year* places Envision, Adani and Suzlon in the top three. That does not establish the same order for cumulative fleet, current order intake or margins. [JMK / C03](https://jmkresearch.com/renewable-sector-published-reports/annual-india-solar-wind-report-card-fy2026/)
- **What remains unproven:** Sustainable competitive advantages in turbine pricing, independently verified lifetime energy yield, order execution speed, service contract economics and return on incremental investment.

**Level 2 preliminary conclusion:** A credible scaled Indian OEM/service platform with a potentially useful integrated project model; competitive durability and the quality of order conversion still require evidence.

## 1. Industry Context & Company Positioning

Level 1 established that India had 59.20 GW wind capacity as of September 2026, FY2025–26 added about 6.06 GW, and demand is shaped by policy, power demand and developer economics. At company level, those tailwinds must pass through a series of commercial and operational gates. [01. Industry Analysis](01-industry-analysis.md)

| Industry condition (Level 1) | Suzlon exposure (Level 2) | Company evidence to track |
| --- | --- | --- |

| Land, approvals and grid bottlenecks | Projects can be delayed even after orders are booked | DevCo readiness, EPC scope, erected-but-not-commissioned MW |
| Commodity, FX and financing pressures | Pricing, procurement and production planning become critical | Contract escalation terms, supplier sourcing, order price mix |

**Central transition:** Industry growth creates the *potential* for Suzlon orders; contract quality and execution determine whether those orders become durable business value.

### Delivery-to-Commissioning Conversion: Diligence Focus

A meaningful gap between **delivered** and **commissioned** MW could partly reflect project schedule and site-readiness constraints, not necessarily manufacturing weakness. But these two figures should not be subtracted to infer an exact delayed backlog without reconciling opening work in process, period recognition, cancellations and geographic coverage.

Do EPC / DevCo orders shorten time-to-commissioning? A project-cohort table is needed before calculating a true backlog conversion rate.
