## Executive Risk Summary & Preliminary Assessment

**Initial conclusion:** Suzlon is no longer in the same financial position as during its historical debt restructuring, but a rapidly expanding, project-dependent wind turbine business can still experience large cash requirements, margin pressure and execution delays. Its growth strategy adds EPC and project-development exposure. The crucial investment risk is not simply weaker wind demand; it is that orders and reported sales grow without matching collections, margins or free cash flow.

**Priority evidence:**

- **Margin risk:** Q2 FY2027 revenue grew 26% year on year, while EBITDA remained around ₹720 crore and quarterly EBITDA margin was 14.8% versus 18.6% one year before. [Company Q2 / R01](https://www.suzlon.com/media/press/press-releases/suzlon-q2-revenue-grows-26-yo-y-on-record-deliveries/)
- **Cash conversion:** FY2026 consolidated CFO (\~₹1,202 crore) was far below PAT (\~₹3,163 crore); cash conversion needs normalization and reconciliation. [Financial history / R02](https://www.screener.in/company/SUZLON/consolidated/) · [FY2026 / R03](https://www.suzlon.com/pdf/green-business/Q4FY26_Results_Release_FINAL.pdf)
- **Liquidity quality:** CRISIL estimated ₹2,599 crore cash and equivalents on June 30, 2026, but only ₹1,392 crore as free cash; other cash was encumbered. [CRISIL / R04](https://www.crisilratings.com/mnt/winshare/Ratings/RatingList/RatingDocs/SuzlonEnergyLimited_October%2027_%202026_RR_406078.html)
- **Execution scope:** Suzlon reported 38% EPC share of the October 2026 order book and an intention to increase EPC share toward 50% by FY2028. More project scope can change margin and risk. [R01](https://www.suzlon.com/media/press/press-releases/suzlon-q2-revenue-grows-26-yo-y-on-record-deliveries/)
- **Credit improvement:** CRISIL upgraded Suzlon to AA-/Stable in October 2026, but its rationale explicitly recounts defaults and restructuring in 2020 and notes financing/working-capital exposure. [R04](https://www.crisilratings.com/mnt/winshare/Ratings/RatingList/RatingDocs/SuzlonEnergyLimited_October%2027_%202026_RR_406078.html)


> ⚖️ **Risk framing**
>
> Financial distress is a historical fact; renewed distress is **not** an established present outcome. The task is to determine whether plausible downside operating events could erode cash flow and valuation—not to assume insolvency.


## 1. Risk Framework, Materiality & Scoring

Use risk = probability × economic impact only when probabilities and impacts can be estimated responsibly. Until then assign a **qualitative priority** and **confidence in the evidence** rather than arbitrary numbers.

| Dimension | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Exposure | What operational or financial variable changes? |
| Transmission mechanism | How does it affect orders, deliveries, price, operating costs, CFO or valuation? |
| Leading indicator | What public data will show deterioration early? |
| Severity | Financial effect if the risk materializes; quantify in model |
| Mitigant | What contracts, cash or capabilities genuinely reduce exposure? |
| Confidence | Are the supporting facts independently verified? |

**Risk prioritization:** “High”, “Medium” and “Low” below are analyst preliminary classifications, not empirically calculated risk probabilities.

## 3. Industry, Policy & Demand Risks

From Level 1, Indian wind deployment grew rapidly in FY2026, but new auctions, PPA signing, debt financing, land rights, transmission completion and commissioning are separate stages.

### What could go wrong?

- Wind tenders proceed but contracts are delayed; developers cannot place firm equipment orders.
- Grid evacuation, land and right-of-way challenges prevent commissioned projects despite delivered equipment.
- Energy buyers favor solar + storage or different hybrid configurations, changing turbine economics.
- Interest-rate, commodity or FX shocks impair developer economics or equipment margins.
- State-level regulation or offtaker payment issues reduce project bankability.

**Monitor:** Actual commissioned GW against tender/award GW; project financing and PPAs; wind component of hybrid procurements; state commissioning trends; and trade-off between tariff levels and project returns. [SECI wind programme / R05](https://seci.co.in/wind) · [India wind market / R06](https://jmkresearch.com/renewable-sector-published-reports/annual-india-solar-wind-report-card-fy2026/)

## 4. Operating, Product & Execution Risks

### 4.1 Backlog is not cash

Suzlon described 6+ GW order book and 710 MW delivered in Q2 FY2027 versus 454 MW commissioned during the same quarter. These measures span different stages; do **not** infer the exact “undelivered backlog” by simply subtracting one metric from another. [R01](https://www.suzlon.com/media/press/press-releases/suzlon-q2-revenue-grows-26-yo-y-on-record-deliveries/)

**Test:** Build project/order cohorts by sign date, client, stage, expected delivery, actual delivery, commissioning, billing milestone and collection.

### 4.2 Larger turbine platforms and warranty exposure

New product rollouts can reduce unit cost but involve certification, field-performance and quality risks. Model a warranty reserve as a function of deliveries, installed cohorts and historical claims; do not assume management-stated energy-yield benefits are independently verified.

### 4.3 EPC mix expansion

Higher EPC order mix can improve customer integration and bring revenues forward, but may create margin, cost-overrun, working-capital and construction/contract liability risk. Determine whether customers remunerate Suzlon adequately for this increased scope.

## 5. Financial Liquidity & Cash Flow Risk

### Liquidity waterfall

1. Start with unrestricted cash and marketable investments, not gross cash alone.
2. Add realistic cash collections and committed available bank limits.
3. Subtract wages, suppliers, capex, tax, interest and scheduled debt payments.
4. Include collateral, guarantees, warranty settlements and contingent payments.
5. Stress customer delays and project execution, then assess minimum liquidity and covenant headroom.

CRISIL notes high reliance on non-fund-based limits (average 79% utilisation over the 12 months through July 2026, as reported). It also reported substantial cash that was encumbered as at June 2026. These can matter even when reported net debt is low. [R04](https://www.crisilratings.com/mnt/winshare/Ratings/RatingList/RatingDocs/SuzlonEnergyLimited_October%2027_%202026_RR_406078.html)

### Cash-flow reversal watch list

| Observable change | Possible implication | Verification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| PAT rises while CFO stagnates | Receivables / contract assets / non-cash earnings | Cash-flow notes and tax reconciliation |
| Inventories expand faster than deliveries | Production mismatch or project schedule change | Inventory composition and delivery data |
| Customer advances shrink | More self-funded project pipeline | Contract liability notes |
| Guarantee requirements rise | Available cash / bank-limit headroom contracts | Bank, contract and rating disclosures |
| Capex rises without productivity improvement | Lower FCFF / return on capital | Capacity utilisation and commissioning |
| Service provisions grow | Quality and warranty expense risk | Warranty/O&M notes |

## 6. Quantitative Stress Tests — Illustrative Mechanics

**These are sensitivity examples, not forecasts and not observed shocks.**

| Test | Reference calculation | Illustrative first-order impact |
| --- | --- | --- |
| EBITDA margin drops by 2 percentage points | 2% × FY2026 revenue ₹16,679 cr | ≈₹334 cr lower annual EBITDA, if revenue is unchanged |
| Receivable collection slows by 30 days | FY2026 revenue ₹16,679 cr / 365 × 30 | ≈₹1,371 cr additional receivables, as a simple sales-based proxy |
| 10% of an illustrative 1 GW delivery cohort delayed | 1,000 MW × 10% | 100 MW shift in delivery timing; revenue / cash effect requires valid realised price and contract timing |
| WACC increases | Revalue same FCFF at a higher discount rate | Enterprise value generally declines; exact change requires DCF |
| Restricted cash rises | Same gross reported cash, lower unrestricted portion | Reduced deployable liquidity; not necessarily immediate P&L loss |

**Warnings:** The 30-day receivables example ignores tax, customer advances, unbilled assets and seasonality. The EBITDA example is pre-tax, before potential cost responses. Do not sum examples as if effects were independent.

## 7. Integrated Operating & Valuation Scenarios

Scenario design must connect **industry demand → win rate → delivery → price/margin → capex and working capital → FCFF → fair value**.

| Driver | Downside | Base | Upside |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Industry installation / project conversion | Grid and customer delays | Deployment recovery broadly continues | Faster land/grid execution and firm procurement |
| Suzlon order quality / delivery | Backlog ages and cancellations rise | Reasonable conversion, some timing variation | Higher delivery throughput and stronger conversion |
| Turbine / EPC pricing | Aggressive competition and raw-material shocks | Mixed price and cost environment | Pricing discipline and cost productivity |
| EBITDA margin | Compressed versus normalized history | Stabilizes after recent pressure | Mix, uptime and scale support improvement |
| Cash collections | Receivables stretch; larger inventory and guarantees | Working capital within historical range | Faster milestones / advance funding |
| Service business | Retention/quality disappoint | Service fleet increases steadily | Strong retention and broader multi-brand contracts |
| Investment requirements | Unplanned capex, warranty or acquisition cost | Planned capacity and maintenance spend | Better utilization without matching capex |
| Resulting cash flows | Weaker or negative FCF | Sustainable but uncertain FCF | Stronger FCFF and capital returns |
| Valuation | Downside EV/share from the same DCF | Base EV/share from verified model | Upside EV/share from verified model |

**Probabilities:** Do not attach 25% / 50% / 25% merely for visual presentation. Assign probabilities only when grounded in evidence, scenario independence and a documented elicitation process. If data are insufficient, use unweighted scenarios and discuss ranges.

## 8. Historical Turnaround & Downside Resilience

Suzlon went through material debt distress and a restructuring in June 2020; CRISIL describes subsequent refinancing in May 2022 and the extinguishment of unsustainable debt via equity conversion. Historical defaults are a fact, but today's bank-facility rating and balance sheet are materially improved. [CRISIL 2026 / R04](https://www.crisilratings.com/mnt/winshare/Ratings/RatingList/RatingDocs/SuzlonEnergyLimited_October%2027_%202026_RR_406078.html) · [Suzlon 2023 placement document / R07](https://www.suzlon.com/NewPdf/Offer_Documents/2022-23/Suzlon_Energy_Limited_-_Placement_Document_dated_August_14%2C_2023.pdf)

### Retrospective questions

- Was pre-2020 distress driven mainly by excessive leverage, industry weakness, poor project execution, acquisitions or a combination?
- Which debt claims were reduced, extended, refinanced or converted into equity, and at what cost to existing shareholders?
- What role did industry recovery and order growth play after the balance-sheet restructuring?
- Did restructuring repair the business economics or only its debt maturity profile?
- Which historical risk channels still exist (working capital, customer concentration, contractual execution)?

**Historical counterfactual research exercise:** Choose an earlier date and use only information then available to ask: refinance, convert debt to equity, sell assets, seek a strategic buyer, or restructure operations? Compare outcomes, dilution, survival and stakeholder incentives without hindsight bias.

**Present-day stress test:** Can a 12–18-month period of weaker delivery and slower collections be funded from truly available resources without major shareholder dilution? This must be modelled before a confidence assessment.

## 9. Decision Thresholds & Thesis-Invalidation Tests

| Decision issue | Working trigger | Required action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Backlog delivery | Persistent fall in delivered MW / cohort conversion despite claimed firm orders | Revisit growth and revenue assumptions |
| Margin deterioration | Sustained margin below normalized plan without credible reversible drivers | Reduce normalized EBIT/FCFF assumptions |
| Cash quality | CFO remains materially below normalized earnings over several periods | Investigate ageing, advances, inventory and accruals |
| Credit / funding | Material reduction in free cash or guarantee headroom | Test liquidity and financing contingency |
| Product failures | Material field reliability or warranty problems | Reassess brand, service renewal and provisions |
| Capital allocation | Major debt-funded diversification or value-dilutive acquisition | Revisit ROIC, net debt, dilution and SOTP |
| Relative valuation | Current price implies growth or returns materially above achievable operating economics | Require stronger margin of safety; do not issue automatic buy |
| Thesis falsification | Multiple core assumptions fail and no longer support positive FCFF | Reject / avoid until evidence changes |

**Threshold discipline:** Set numerical cutoffs only after establishing historically meaningful ranges and a finished model. “A 2% margin hit” is not automatically an invest/avoid trigger independent of valuation.

## 11. Analytical Frameworks & Risk Methodology

| Framework | Role |
| --- | --- |
| Enterprise risk register | Classify risks by exposure, impact, mitigant and evidence |
| Sensitivity analysis | Identify high-impact model inputs |
| Bear / Base / Bull integrated scenario model | Represent coherent alternative operating outcomes |
| Reverse stress test | Find conditions that break liquidity or investment case |
| Liquidity runway / 13-week cash forecast | Relevant for severe cash stress or counterfactual turnaround, not required in every ordinary equity model |
| Historical restructuring / counterfactual | Evaluate resilience and capital structure lessons |
| Decision-tree or Monte Carlo | Advanced tools only when driver distributions and dependencies are credible |
| Monitoring / catalyst dashboard | Make investment thesis observable and falsifiable |

## 14. Investment Decision Handoff

**Evidence-led conclusion:** The company has stronger credit quality and operating scale, but the sensitivity of cash and earnings to delivery delays, working capital and competition warrants caution. The risk register supports further analysis rather than an immediate invest/reject verdict.

**Decision requirements:** Complete normalized Level 3 cash bridge; value the same scenarios in Level 4; identify evidence that would invalidate the thesis; record final evidence and alternatives in the investment committee memo.
