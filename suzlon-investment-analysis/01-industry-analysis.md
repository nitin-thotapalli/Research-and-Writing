## Executive Summary & Preliminary Industry Outlook

**Preliminary view:** India's wind industry appears to have strong structural demand drivers, but the attractiveness of the wind-turbine OEM business is conditional on converting policy ambition and awarded projects into commissioned turbines, collecting cash, controlling manufacturing costs and sustaining service economics. A growing industry does not automatically mean attractive OEM returns.

- **Scale:** 59.20 GW of installed wind capacity as of 30 September 2026, within 558.90 GW of total Indian power-generation capacity. Wind is roughly 10.6% of installed capacity, *not* necessarily the same share of electricity generated. [MNRE / S01](https://mnre.gov.in/documents/guidelines/physical-achievements-kzN2kTMtQWa?pageTitle=Physical-Achievements) · [NITI Aayog / S02](https://iced.niti.gov.in/)
- **Recent growth:** Wind additions rose from 3.253 GW in FY2023–24 to 4.151 GW in FY2024–25 and 6.057 GW in FY2025–26. Wind generation in FY2025–26 was approximately 106 billion units (BU), compared with 83 BU in each of the prior two years. [PIB / S03](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2287725&lang=1&reg=3)
- **Industry ambition:** The government has articulated a 100 GW wind-capacity ambition by 2030. That is a policy ambition, not an assured installed-capacity forecast. [PIB / S04](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleaseIframePage.aspx?PRID=2320750&lang=2&reg=48)
- **Near-term uncertainty:** Transmission availability, land, right-of-way, PPA finalisation, financing and the shift to hybrid/firm power procurement can delay projects. [GWEC / S05](https://marketintelligence.gwec.net/country-profiles/india/) · [IEA / S06](https://www.iea.org/reports/india-renewable-energy-market-report-2026/executive-summary)
- **Initial conclusion:** *Attractive growth opportunity; industry profitability and value capture are not yet established.* Treat this as a working hypothesis to test through the evidence and company research.

## 1. Industry Scope & Market Structure

| Layer | What it includes | Relevance to Suzlon |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Indian energy system | Coal, gas, nuclear, hydro, solar, wind; transmission, distribution and consumers | Determines electricity demand, system costs, regulation and the competing supply options |
| Renewable electricity | Solar, wind, hydro, biomass, storage and hybrid/firm supply | Establishes how wind competes and complements other generation sources |
| Wind power industry | Resource development, project financing, land, grid connection, commissioning and electricity sales | Determines timing and bankability of turbine orders |
| Wind turbine manufacturing & services | Turbine design, components, assembly, EPC interfaces, warranties, operations and maintenance (O&M) | The specific profit pool to investigate for Suzlon |

**Key actors:** Government and regulators set incentives and rules; procurement agencies and buyers contract electricity; project developers arrange land, financing and equipment; OEMs supply wind turbines; transmission owners evacuate electricity; service providers maintain operating assets.

**Important distinction:** Suzlon primarily participates in turbine equipment, execution and services. It should not be valued using the same business economics as a utility that owns wind farms and sells electricity under PPAs.

**Macroeconomic transmission mechanism:** Electricity demand influences capacity needs → tariffs and the cost of capital determine project bankability → financing plus land and grid access enable construction → OEMs receive turbine orders → delivery, commissioning and cash collection create actual manufacturer revenue and cash flow. No one macro variable directly guarantees Suzlon profits.

## 5. Policy, Regulation & Market Mechanisms

### 5.1 Power Procurement & Contracting Framework

SECI runs competitive project tenders, selects renewable developers, signs long-term power purchase agreements with developers and typically back-to-back power sale agreements with electricity distribution buyers. Its wind overview describes 25-year PPAs in its framework. [SECI / S12](https://seci.co.in/wind)

**Conversion ladder:** Government ambition → tender issued → developer award → PPA / PSA → financing → land and grid approvals → OEM order → turbine manufacturing → commissioning → operating electricity generation → payment collection.

Different points in this ladder may be delayed or cancelled. An award does not become OEM revenue automatically.

### 5.2 Renewable Consumption Obligation (RCO): Regulatory Framework

The Ministry of Power's **27 September 2025** gazette replaced the October 2023 notification for the covered framework. It sets a total renewable consumption trajectory of **35.95% for FY2026–27** and **43.33% for FY2029–30**, with a specified wind component. The rules include fungibility between some technology categories; therefore, a nominal wind component is not equivalent to guaranteed demand for new wind turbines. [Official revised gazette / S13](https://powermin.gov.in/sites/default/files/Revised_RCO_Gazzette_Notification_dated_27th_September_2025.pdf)

| Financial year | Total renewable obligation | Wind category |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2025–26 | 33.01% | 1.45% |
| 2026–27 | 35.95% | 1.97% |
| 2027–28 | 38.81% | 2.45% |
| 2028–29 | 41.36% | 2.95% |
| 2029–30 | 43.33% | 3.48% |

**Research caution:** Distinguish legal obligation, permitted substitution / REC compliance and actual incremental wind procurement.

### 5.3 Manufacturing Standards & Localisation Requirements

India operates an Approved List of Models and Manufacturers for wind turbines and related component rules. Model certification and domestic manufacturing arrangements matter for eligible installations. [MNRE wind quality / S14](https://ccdcwind.gov.in/)

Government sources quote approximately **24 GW** of wind-turbine manufacturing capacity as of March 2026, while SECI's published industry overview cites **18 GW annually**. These figures use potentially different definitions or reference dates. They are **not reconciled**; do not calculate utilisation from either without confirming scope. [PIB / S07](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2272630&lang=2&reg=48) · [SECI / S12](https://seci.co.in/wind)

## 6. Market Sizing & Addressable Opportunity

**Rule:** Installed base, annual installations, available projects, turbines ordered, OEM sales, total project capex and O&M services are not interchangeable.

| Measure | 2026 reference / methodology | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Installed wind fleet | 59.20 GW at Sep 2026 | Existing assets; important for repowering and O&M, not current-year equipment sales |
| FY2025–26 additions | 6.057 GW | Actual commissioned annual additions; better volume indicator for the turbine market |
| FY2026–27 additions | 6.9 GW forecast by JMK | Third-party estimate, not actual installation; [JMK / S15](https://jmkresearch.com/renewable-sector-published-reports/annual-india-solar-wind-report-card-fy2026/) |
| Government 2030 ambition | 100 GW installed | Policy goal; implies approximately 40.8 GW of additional installed wind capacity above Sep 2026, before adjustments |
| Wind resource potential | SECI cites 695.5 GW at 120 m and 1,163.9 GW at 150 m | Technical potential, **not** financially addressable market. [SECI / S12](https://seci.co.in/wind) |
| Project-capex market | Annual new MW × all-in developed-project capex/MW | Includes more than wind turbines; do not attribute entire number to OEMs |
| OEM equipment TAM | Addressable newly supplied MW × verified realised turbine-equipment ASP/MW | Build only after checking contract mix, EPC exclusions and comparable sales |
| Services / O&M TAM | Installed fleet under service × verified annual service revenue/MW | Contract coverage, vintage and pricing assumptions needed |

### TAM, SAM & SOM: Methodology & Definitions

- **TAM (Total Addressable Market):** Total annual Indian wind-turbine equipment and related service opportunity, measured in ₹ using disclosed project/OEM economics.
- **SAM (Serviceable Addressable Market):** Indian turbine projects compatible with eligible turbine models, customer segments, geography, capacity and execution capabilities.
- **SOM (Serviceable Obtainable Market):** A company-specific attainable share given competitive awards, delivery capability, installed base, order book and conversion.
- **Unit conversions:** 1 GW = 1,000 MW. An MW figure multiplied by ₹ crore/MW yields ₹ crore. Use a separate assumption for manufacturing ASP, EPC and long-term service revenue.
- **Current research gap:** An audited, like-for-like turbine OEM selling price / MW has not yet been validated, so a precise ₹ market-size estimate would be premature.

### Tender Pipeline & Project Conversion Analysis

SECI's wind tender tracker (as of 30 October 2026, for the listed SECI tranches) shows approximately 23.05 GW tendered, 17.85 GW awarded and 7.38 GW commissioned. This is **only the listed SECI programme**, not the full Indian wind-market backlog or all developer projects. Timing and programme eligibility affect comparisons. [SECI / S12](https://seci.co.in/wind)

**Research implication:** Measure tender → PPA → project → turbine order → installation conversion over time before forecasting manufacturers' revenue.

## 7. Industry Value Chain & Profit Pool Analysis

| Actor | Economic model | Main sources of margin / value | Main vulnerabilities |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Component suppliers | Blades, towers, gearboxes, generators, castings, power electronics | Product margins, scale, quality, specialised know-how | Commodity inflation, buyer concentration, capacity / warranty costs |
| Wind turbine OEM | Design, manufacture and supply turbine systems | Equipment ASP minus materials, labour, warranty, logistics and overhead | Price pressure, model certification, delivery schedules and working capital |
| EPC / balance of plant | Foundations, roads, electrical works, installation, grid interfaces | Project execution spread, engineering capabilities | Land, permitting, civil-cost overruns and schedule slippage |
| Renewable developer / IPP | Acquires, finances and operates projects; sells power | Contracted tariff × actual generation less operating / finance costs | Financing cost, plant load factor, payments, grid curtailment |
| Transmission / grid operators | Network access and power evacuation | Regulated / contracted network economics | Network congestion, project sequencing |
| O&M service provider | Multi-year maintenance, uptime and spare-parts services | Contract fees, reliability, installed fleet, service efficiency | Warranty claims, downtime, pricing, legacy fleet complexity |

**Customer economics to investigate:** Developer economics depend on tariff, capacity factor, delivered energy, project capex, debt cost and collection risk. OEM economics depend on order price, component sourcing, plant utilisation, execution, warranty costs and cash conversion. These are related but distinct.

**Product-analyst lens:** Track reliability/availability, energy yield, on-time delivery, installation cycle time, service contract attachment, customer concentration, warranty claims and repeat-order rates as operational drivers of business value.

### Porter's Five Forces: Preliminary Assessment

| Force | Tentative intensity | Why | Test with |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Rivalry among OEMs | High | Multiple domestic and international suppliers compete for developer orders; buyers compare price and performance. | Tender outcomes, win rates, realised ASP, margin trends |
| Buyer bargaining power | Medium–high | Large developers procure significant volumes and can negotiate technology, guarantees and terms. | Customer concentration, contract size, deposit terms |
| Supplier bargaining power | Medium / selective high | Some components are replaceable; complex quality-certified parts may have limited approved alternatives. | Supplier concentration, lead times, localisation |
| Threat of new entrants | Medium | Manufacturing investment, certification, service network and references are barriers, yet new suppliers can enter. | Approved model lists, new commissioning share |
| Substitutes | Medium–high | Solar and storage compete for renewable capex; hybrid/firm power can also create complementary demand for wind. | Comparative levelised costs, generation profile and tenders |

These ratings are **analyst hypotheses, not measured scores**. Validate with tender, pricing and financial evidence before treating them as conclusions.

## 9. Industry Maturity, Growth Drivers & Scenarios

**Life-cycle assessment:** Established industry with renewed capacity-addition growth, alongside a shift toward larger turbine platforms, hybrid power and greater system integration. Not an unqualified early-stage boom.

### Structural Demand Drivers

1. Rising electricity use and demand for energy security.
2. National renewable and wind-capacity ambitions plus consumption obligations.
3. Hybrid and firm/dispatchable renewable tenders that combine resources.
4. Domestic manufacturing ecosystem and localisation policies.
5. Repowering old turbines and the O&M requirements of a growing installed fleet.

### Structural Constraints & Execution Risks

1. Slow land acquisition, local approvals and right-of-way resolution.
2. Grid availability, curtailment, evacuation and transmission investment timing.
3. Developer financing cost and DISCOM/offtaker bankability.
4. Delays between tenders, PPAs, project readiness and commissioning.
5. Turbine pricing competition and component/warranty inflation.
6. Technological change, reliability, resource variability and contract execution risk.

The IEA's November 2026 India Renewable Energy Market Report highlights grid/storage integration, DISCOM finances, land and permitting as key barriers to further renewable deployment. [IEA / S06](https://www.iea.org/reports/india-renewable-energy-market-report-2026/executive-summary)

### Industry Scenario Analysis: Qualitative Framework

| Scenario | Conditions | Wind market effect | OEM implication |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Downside | Delayed PPAs, expensive financing, weak grid access, margin pressure | Projects slip and annual installations disappoint | Order conversion and cash flow weaken even if headline backlog stays high |
| Base | Procurement and commissioning remain broadly aligned with the recent recovery | Deployment grows but unevenly across states and periods | Capable OEMs benefit, but competition and working capital constrain returns |
| Upside | Faster transmission, clearer project pipeline, strong hybrid/C&I demand, efficient execution | More rapid commissioning and improved visibility | Higher volume and factory utilisation; value still depends on pricing and product quality |

**Monitor rather than assume:** The 100 GW policy ambition needs about 40.8 GW added beyond the Sep 2026 installed base. We must test whether land, project pipeline and annual commissioning capacity can support that trajectory. This is an ambition-to-capacity arithmetic gap, **not** a guaranteed order forecast.

> 🧠 **Working industry thesis — not an investment recommendation**
>
> India offers meaningful potential for wind-turbine deployment as power demand expands and procurement moves toward renewables and hybrid supply. However, project execution bottlenecks and OEM competition can prevent industry volume growth from converting into shareholder value. The decisive question for Suzlon is whether it can turn this opportunity into sustainable margins, cash flow and returns on invested capital at an attractive valuation.

## 14. Key Findings & Investment Implications

**What we know with reasonable confidence:** Wind deployment has recovered sharply; public targets imply continuing demand; grid, land and contracting are material constraints; wind turbine makers face meaningful competition.

**What we do not yet know:** The true ₹ value of the addressable turbine market, sector-wide OEM operating margins, return on capital, actual vendor manufacturing utilisation, the quality of future orders, and whether Suzlon commands lasting pricing power.

**Next question for Stage 02 — Business Analysis:** Does Suzlon possess a durable advantage in products, installation execution, customer access and O&M services that lets it outperform the industry economically?
