## Executive Summary & Valuation Status

**Initial assessment:** Suzlon trades as a growing renewable-energy equipment and services company following an operating recovery and balance-sheet restructuring. A meaningful fair-value estimate requires normalized segment profitability, a defensible working-capital forecast, audited debt/cash adjustments, an appropriate share count and a WACC/terminal-value analysis. None of these should be inferred merely from the order book or the government's wind-capacity targets.

**Valuation status: INCOMPLETE — no price target or investment rating issued.** The market-price reference and preliminary trading-multiple observations below are factual or arithmetical checks, *not* a claim that Suzlon is cheap or expensive. ## 2. Valuation Methodology & Triangulation

| Method | Application | Evidence requirement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Discounted Cash Flow (FCFF) | Primary method for core operating assets | Driver-based forecast, capital investment, working capital, WACC |
| Trading Comps / Relative Valuation | Market benchmark for comparable wind OEM economics | Comparable revenue/EPC/O&M mix and period alignment |
| Reverse DCF | Determines expectations required to justify observed market enterprise value | Verified EV and normalized starting free cash flow |
| Sum-of-the-Parts (SOTP) | Potentially useful if turbine, EPC and O&M economics are sufficiently separable | Reliable financial segment reporting and intercompany eliminations |
| Asset-based / replacement approach | Cross-check only where economically relevant | Asset quality, liabilities and earnings power; not automatic “floor” |

**Never average mismatched values mechanically.** The primary method and confidence should reflect what the business actually earns and the quality of each data series.

## 4. Three-Statement Financial Model

### Profit & Loss assumptions

Revenue by segment → operating expenses → EBITDA → depreciation/amortisation → EBIT → interest/other non-operating items → tax → normalized earnings.

### Balance Sheet assumptions

Net fixed assets roll forward with capital expenditure, depreciation, disposals and acquisitions. Operating working capital flows through receivables, inventory, contract assets, advances and payables. Include lease obligations, financing facilities and restrictions.

### Cash Flow assumptions

Operating cash flow = after-tax operating profit + non-cash items − net working-capital investment (subject to accounting reconciliations).

Investment cash flow includes sustaining and expansion capex, acquisitions and disposals. Financing cash flow captures debt, interest, equity issuance and distributions.

**Required integrity checks:** Assets = liabilities + equity; opening cash + net movements = closing cash; financial-year balance sheet ties to year-end cash flow; future share count reconciles issued and diluted equity; use one currency and sign convention.

## 5. Discounted Cash Flow (DCF) — FCFF

**Preferred working formula:**

FCFF = EBIT × (1 − normalized marginal cash tax rate) + depreciation & amortisation − capital expenditure − increase in operating net working capital.

**Enterprise value:** Present value of forecast FCFF + present value of terminal value.

**WACC:** Weight the after-tax cost of debt and cost of equity using defensible target capital structure. Determine risk-free rate, equity risk premium and beta from consistent market data. A historic debt crisis does not justify using an arbitrary high discount rate without modelling the changed capital structure and risk profile.

**Terminal value sensitivity:** Use a sustainable long-run growth rate bounded by real-world conditions. Check implied terminal ROIC, reinvestment, margin and terminal EV/EBITDA rather than accepting the spreadsheet result uncritically.

## 6. Enterprise Value to Equity Value Bridge

| Component | Treatment |
| --- | --- |
| PV of operating business | DCF enterprise value |
| Add: non-operating cash / investments | Only amounts not required for operations and legitimately attributable to equity |
| Subtract: interest-bearing financial debt and leases | Avoid double-counting costs and debt financing |
| Subtract: noncontrolling interests and other material claims | Match EV scope to consolidated earnings |
| Consider: restricted deposits / guarantees | Not automatically distributable surplus |
| Adjust: other non-operating assets / liabilities | Only with evidence and no double counting |
| Divide by: fully diluted current share count | Latest shares, options, warrants and other potential dilution |

## 8. Reverse Valuation & Implied Expectations

This is a particularly useful independent analytical exercise.

1. Start with a reconciled enterprise value at the chosen share price.
2. Estimate normalized current operating profit and recurring FCFF.
3. Solve for the future delivery growth, operating margin and working-capital intensity that would justify that enterprise value.
4. Compare those implied operating requirements with manufacturing capacity, order-book cohorts, market growth and achievable customer demand.
5. Ask whether the market is paying for already demonstrated delivery capability or assuming successful strategy execution.

**Candidate research question:** “How much of Suzlon's future business-model expansion must succeed to justify the quoted valuation?” This can generate an original investment insight without claiming a false-precision price target.
